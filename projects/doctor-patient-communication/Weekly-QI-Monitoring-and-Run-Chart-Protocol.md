# Weekly QI Monitoring & Run Chart Interpretation Protocol

## Improving Doctor–Patient Communication Through Patient Voice

**Project:** Patient Voice for Quality Care  
**Implementing Organization:** Quality Care Concern  
**Pilot Setting:** Hospital Outpatient Department (OPD)  
**Pilot Duration:** 12 Weeks  
**Document Version:** 1.0 — Draft  
**Document Type:** Quality Improvement Monitoring and Evaluation Protocol

---

## 1. Purpose

This protocol provides a standardized approach to monitoring, analysing, interpreting, and reporting patient-reported experiences of doctor–patient communication during a 12-week hospital OPD quality improvement pilot.

It aims to help healthcare teams distinguish routine variation from possible improvement, identify emerging communication problems, and make evidence-informed decisions through Plan–Do–Study–Act (PDSA) cycles.

## 2. Monitoring Objectives

1. Track weekly performance of three patient-reported communication indicators.
2. Establish and document baseline performance.
3. Identify patterns and variation over time.
4. Assess whether improvement interventions are associated with changes in patient experience.
5. Identify unintended consequences and operational challenges.
6. Support continuous learning and improvement.

## 3. Core Outcome Indicators

| Code | Indicator | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Understandable Explanation | Percentage of valid respondents reporting that the doctor's explanation was easy to understand |
| M2 | Active Listening | Percentage of valid respondents reporting that the doctor listened attentively |
| M3 | Opportunity for Questions | Percentage of valid respondents reporting that they had an opportunity to ask questions |

### Calculation

**Positive Response Rate (%) = (Number of Positive Responses / Number of Valid Responses) × 100**

For the proposed pilot:

- Yes = Positive
- Partly = Non-positive
- No = Non-positive
- Not applicable / No response = Excluded from the indicator denominator

All calculations must follow the approved Measurement Plan, Questionnaire, Indicator Dictionary, and Excel Dashboard.

## 4. Weekly Data Collection and Validation

Patient feedback should be collected after OPD consultations using a standardized questionnaire.

### Weekly Procedures

1. Collect patient responses throughout the reporting week.
2. Enter responses into the designated Excel Data Entry Sheet.
3. Review entries for missing values, duplicates, and invalid response codes.
4. Calculate indicator-specific numerators and denominators.
5. Update the Weekly Summary and Dashboard.
6. Review findings during the weekly QI meeting.
7. Document decisions and follow-up actions.

### Data Quality Checks

- Confirm the reporting week and dates.
- Check that each eligible response is recorded once.
- Confirm that the numerator does not exceed the denominator.
- Exclude invalid or missing responses according to the approved scoring rules.
- Record weekly sample sizes and response rates.
- Investigate unusual changes before drawing conclusions.

## 6. Run Chart Construction

Prepare a separate run chart for each indicator or a combined chart with clearly distinguishable series.

### Chart Requirements

- X-axis: Sequential reporting weeks.
- Y-axis: Positive Response Rate (%), from 0 to 100.
- Display M1, M2, and M3 with clear labels.
- Display missing observations as gaps, not as zero.
- Record the numerator and denominator for each weekly point.
- Annotate major interventions and relevant contextual events.
- Use a median reference line when sufficient data are available.

A median calculated from only two baseline weeks is not sufficient to establish a reliable run-chart reference for formal signal detection.

## 7. Run Chart Interpretation

Run charts are used to identify patterns in data collected over time.

### 7.1 Shift

A shift is a sustained sequence of points on one side of the reference median.

A commonly used healthcare QI convention is six or more consecutive non-median points on the same side of the median.

The exact rule and required number of observations should follow a specified, consistently applied run-chart methodology.

### 7.2 Trend

A trend is a sustained sequence of consecutive increases or decreases.

A commonly used convention is five or more consecutive points moving in one direction.

Equal consecutive values are not counted as increases or decreases.

### 7.3 Runs

A run is a sequence of consecutive observations on the same side of the median.

Too few or too many runs may suggest non-random variation, depending on the number of usable observations and the selected statistical rule.

### 7.4 Astronomical Point

An astronomical point is an observation that is visibly unusual compared with the other data.

This is a subjective signal and should be interpreted cautiously.

### Important Interpretation Limitations

- Two baseline observations are insufficient for reliable run-chart signal testing.
- Weekly percentages may fluctuate substantially when denominators are small.
- A higher percentage in one week does not necessarily establish improvement.
- When enough observations accumulate, the QI team should use a predefined run-chart methodology.
- Formal statistical process control may require a longer series and appropriate chart selection.

|---|---|---|

|---|---|

Balancing measures should be reviewed alongside outcome improvements.

## 12. Decision-Making Framework

| Observation | Suggested Response |
|---|---|
| Sustained improvement supported by adequate data | Consider adopting or expanding the change |
| No clear improvement | Review implementation fidelity and test an adaptation |
| Deteriorating patient feedback | Investigate potential causes promptly |
| Increased waiting time or staff burden | Reassess the intervention and workflow |
| Missing or inconsistent data | Resolve data quality issues before interpretation |
| Insufficient observations | Continue measurement without claiming a statistical signal |

## 13. End-of-Pilot Evaluation

At Week 12, evaluate:

- Baseline and final M1, M2, and M3 performance.
- Absolute percentage-point changes.
- Weekly run-chart patterns.
- Number and outcomes of PDSA cycles.
- Process measure performance.
- Balancing measure findings.
- Data completeness and response rates.
- Patient and staff feedback.
- Feasibility and sustainability.

### Reporting Requirements

For each indicator, present the numerator, denominator, percentage, and measurement period.

Report both improvements and limitations.

A difference between baseline and final percentages alone does not establish causation.

## 14. Sustainability and Scale-Up

If results support continued implementation, the QI team should consider:

- Incorporating effective communication practices into routine OPD procedures.
- Orienting newly assigned clinical staff.
- Continuing periodic patient feedback monitoring.
- Reviewing performance through established clinical governance meetings.
- Testing the approach in additional OPD departments.
- Adapting the toolkit for different hospital contexts.

Scale-up decisions should consider feasibility, resource requirements, patient diversity, and evidence of sustained improvement.

## 15. Data Protection and Responsible Use

This project should use anonymized or appropriately de-identified information for public reporting.

Do not upload identifiable patient-level data, confidential medical records, or staff performance information to a public GitHub repository.

Publicly shared dashboards should contain only approved, non-identifiable data.

## 16. Limitations

This protocol is a proposed quality improvement monitoring framework, not a validated clinical assessment instrument.

The initial two-week baseline is intended for descriptive planning. Formal interpretation of run-chart signals requires sufficient sequential observations.

Findings from a single OPD pilot may not be generalizable to other settings without additional testing.
