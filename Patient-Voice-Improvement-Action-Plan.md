# Patient Voice Improvement Action Plan

## 1. Purpose

This template helps healthcare organisations translate patient feedback into practical, measurable, and sustainable quality improvement actions.

Collecting patient feedback is only the first step. Organisations must analyse what patients say, identify opportunities for improvement, involve patients and frontline staff in designing solutions, implement changes, and assess whether those changes improve the experience of care.

This action plan can be used by hospitals, clinics, outpatient departments, primary healthcare facilities, and other healthcare services.

## 2. Guiding Principles

Patient voice improvement activities should:

- Treat patients with respect and dignity.
- Recognise patients as partners in improving healthcare quality.
- Prioritise safety, accessibility, equity, and patient-centred care.
- Use feedback to improve systems rather than simply blame individuals.
- Include patients and frontline staff in developing solutions.
- Establish clear responsibilities, timelines, and measures.
- Communicate actions and results back to patients.
- Protect patient confidentiality throughout the process.

## 9. Step 7: Test Changes Using PDSA

Use Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA) cycles to test improvements on a manageable scale before wider implementation.

### Plan

- What change will be tested?
- What do we predict will happen?
- Who will participate?
- Where and when will the test take place?
- What data will be collected?

### Do

- Conduct the test on the planned scale.
- Record what happened.
- Document unexpected events and practical difficulties.

### Study

- Compare the results with the prediction.
- Review patient feedback and relevant measures.
- Discuss what worked and what did not.
- Identify limitations in the data.

### Act

- Adopt the change, adapt it, or abandon it.
- Plan the next test if needed.
- Update the action plan and communicate learning to relevant stakeholders.

Review progress at an agreed frequency. Use consistent definitions and collection methods when comparing results over time.

Do not interpret a single favourable result as proof of sustained improvement. Consider sample size, variation over time, data quality, and other changes that may have influenced the findings.

## 11. Step 9: Close the Feedback Loop

Patients should be able to see that their feedback has been heard and considered.

**What did patients tell us?**

**What did the organisation learn?**

**What changes were introduced?**

**What has changed as a result?**

**What remains unresolved?**

**How will patients be informed?**

Possible communication channels include:

- Patient information boards
- Posters in clinical areas
- Website or social media updates
- Patient advisory group meetings
- Follow-up communications where appropriate
- Staff briefings and service improvement reports

Use clear, accessible language. Do not disclose identifiable patient information when sharing examples or findings.

## 12. Step 10: Sustain and Spread Improvements

When results suggest that a change is beneficial:

- Document the revised process.
- Agree who will maintain the change.
- Integrate it into routine workflows where appropriate.
- Orient relevant staff to the new process.
- Continue monitoring the outcome and balancing measures.
- Review whether the improvement is sustained over time.
- Consider testing the change in other departments or services.
- Adapt the approach to local circumstances rather than assuming it will work identically everywhere.

## 14. Final Checklist

Before closing or continuing the improvement project, confirm that:

- [ ] The issue is supported by patient feedback or other relevant evidence.
- [ ] The improvement priority reflects patient needs and risks.
- [ ] Patients or patient representatives have been involved where feasible.
- [ ] The aim statement is specific and measurable.
- [ ] Potential causes have been explored.
- [ ] Each action has a responsible person and deadline.
- [ ] Measures have clear operational definitions.
- [ ] Changes have been tested and evaluated.
- [ ] Data quality and equity considerations have been reviewed.
- [ ] Unintended consequences have been considered.
- [ ] Findings and actions have been communicated to patients.
- [ ] A plan exists to sustain and monitor improvements.

## 15. Key Principle

Patient feedback creates value when it leads to meaningful change.

A strong patient voice improvement process does not stop at collecting survey responses or counting complaints. It connects listening, analysis, action, measurement, learning, and communication into a continuous cycle of improvement.

**Listen to patients. Understand the problem. Test changes. Measure the impact. Close the feedback loop.**
