## 1. Background and Purpose

Effective doctor–patient communication is an essential component of patient-centred healthcare.

This baseline assessment aims to measure patients' reported experiences of communication during outpatient consultations and identify opportunities for quality improvement.

The findings will guide the selection of interventions and subsequent Plan–Do–Study–Act (PDSA) cycles.

## 2. Assessment Objectives

1. Establish baseline performance for three doctor–patient communication indicators.
2. Identify priority communication gaps.
3. Examine variations in patient experiences, where sufficient data are available.
4. Inform a measurable improvement aim and intervention plan.
5. Establish a reference point for monitoring improvement over time.

## 3. Measurement Framework

| Code | Indicator | Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Understandable Explanation | Percentage of valid respondents reporting that the doctor's explanation was easy to understand |
| M2 | Active Listening | Percentage of valid respondents reporting that the doctor listened attentively |
| M3 | Opportunity for Questions | Percentage of valid respondents reporting that they had an opportunity to ask questions |

### Indicator Formula

Positive Response Rate (%) = (Number of Positive Responses / Number of Valid Responses) × 100

For this proposed pilot, "Yes" is classified as positive, while "Partly" and "No" are classified as non-positive.

"Not applicable", refusals, and missing responses are excluded from the respective indicator denominator.

The final scoring system must match the approved questionnaire, Indicator Dictionary, and Excel Dashboard.
