# Quality Improvement Project Charter

## Improving Doctor–Patient Communication Through Patient Voice

**Project Type:** Adaptable Healthcare Quality Improvement Model  
**Project Status:** Proposed / Not Yet Implemented  
**QI Methodology:** Model for Improvement and Plan–Do–Study–Act (PDSA) Cycles  
**Suggested Duration:** 12 Weeks  
**Suggested Setting:** Hospital Outpatient Department (OPD)  
**Project Repository:** [Patient Voice for Quality Care](https://github.com/aurlup-alt/patient-voice-for-quality-care)

## 1. Project Overview

Effective communication between doctors and patients is fundamental to patient-centred, safe, respectful, and high-quality healthcare.

Patients should have opportunities to express their concerns, receive understandable explanations, ask questions, and participate in decisions about their care.

This project provides a structured quality improvement model for healthcare organisations seeking to strengthen doctor–patient communication through systematic patient feedback, measurable indicators, and iterative improvement cycles.

The model is intended to be adapted and tested in local clinical settings. It does not represent findings from an implemented project.

## 2. Problem Statement

Communication problems may occur when patients feel that doctors do not listen carefully, provide insufficient explanations, use unfamiliar medical terminology, or do not create opportunities for questions.

These problems can contribute to misunderstanding, reduced confidence, poor care experiences, and difficulty following treatment instructions.

However, the nature and magnitude of communication gaps must be established through local baseline assessment rather than assumed.

**Local problem statement to complete after baseline assessment:**

> In [name of service or department], [baseline percentage] of surveyed patients reported [specific communication concern] during [measurement period]. The improvement team will address this gap through structured communication practices and patient-informed improvement cycles.

## 3. Project Aim

### Overall Aim

To improve patients' reported experience of doctor–patient communication in a selected clinical service through patient feedback, structured communication practices, and continuous quality improvement.

### Illustrative SMART Aim

To increase the percentage of surveyed patients who report that their doctor's explanation was easy to understand from an established baseline of [X%] to [locally agreed target%] within 12 weeks of implementation.

The target must be determined after baseline measurement and assessment of local feasibility.

## 4. Project Objectives

1. Assess patients' experiences of communication during clinical consultations.
2. Identify priority communication gaps through quantitative and qualitative patient feedback.
3. Engage doctors, nurses, patients, and quality improvement personnel in understanding the causes of identified gaps.
4. Develop and test practical communication interventions using PDSA cycles.
5. Monitor changes in patient experience, implementation fidelity, and potential unintended effects.
6. Establish a feedback-to-improvement process that can be sustained and adapted across healthcare settings.

## 5. Project Scope

### In Scope

- Doctor–patient communication during outpatient consultations.
- Active listening and respectful interactions.
- Clarity of clinical explanations.
- Opportunities for patients to ask questions.
- Checking patients' understanding of instructions and next steps.
- Patient-reported experience measurement.
- Small-scale testing of communication improvements.

### Out of Scope

- Assessment of individual doctors for punitive or disciplinary purposes.
- Evaluation of clinical competence or diagnostic accuracy.
- Disclosure of identifiable patient or clinician information.
- Claims of improved clinical outcomes without appropriate measurement.
- Organisation-wide implementation before local testing and evaluation.

## 6. Target Population and Setting

**Suggested Setting:** One outpatient department or clinical service.

**Target Population:** Patients receiving consultations during the selected measurement periods.

**Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria:** To be defined locally according to the clinical setting, accessibility needs, consent requirements, and approved data collection procedures.

Appropriate assistance should be available for patients with communication, literacy, language, or disability-related barriers.

## 8. Measurement Framework

The following indicators are proposed for local adaptation.

### Outcome Measures

**Indicator 1: Understandable Explanation Rate**

- **Definition:** Percentage of eligible survey respondents reporting that the doctor's explanation was easy to understand.
- **Numerator:** Number of eligible respondents selecting a predefined positive response.
- **Denominator:** Number of eligible respondents providing a valid answer to the question.
- **Data Source:** Patient experience survey.
- **Frequency:** Weekly or at another locally feasible interval.

**Indicator 2: Patient-Reported Listening Experience**

- **Definition:** Percentage of eligible respondents reporting that the doctor listened carefully to their concerns.
- **Numerator:** Number of valid positive responses.
- **Denominator:** Number of valid responses to the relevant question.
- **Data Source:** Patient experience survey.

### Process Measures

**Indicator 3: Opportunity to Ask Questions**

- **Definition:** Percentage of eligible respondents reporting that they were given an opportunity to ask questions.
- **Data Source:** Patient experience survey.

**Indicator 4: Teach-Back Use**

- **Definition:** Percentage of eligible observed or documented consultations in which an appropriate understanding-check technique was used.
- **Numerator:** Number of eligible consultations with documented or observed use.
- **Denominator:** Total eligible consultations assessed.
- **Data Source:** Locally approved observation or documentation process.

### Balancing Measures

- Consultation duration.
- Staff-reported workload or workflow disruption.
- Patient-reported consultation delays, where relevant.

### Measurement Notes

- Use consistent question wording and response categories over time.
- Define positive responses before data collection.
- Report valid denominators and missing responses.
- Review sampling and nonresponse bias.
- Stratify findings by relevant equity dimensions when ethically appropriate and sufficiently safe.
- Do not interpret a locally developed survey as a validated PREMs instrument without appropriate validation.

## 9. Baseline Assessment

Before introducing interventions, the team should:

1. Confirm organisational permissions and applicable ethical requirements.
2. Select and pilot an appropriate patient experience questionnaire.
3. Define a feasible sampling strategy and measurement period.
4. Collect baseline patient feedback.
5. Analyse communication-related responses and qualitative comments.
6. Identify recurring concerns and potential differences between patient groups.
7. Review findings with patients and frontline staff.
8. Establish an improvement target and measurement plan.

**Baseline Status:** Not yet collected.

No baseline values or improvement results are claimed in this model.

## 10. Proposed Change Interventions

Potential interventions include:

### A. Active Listening

Encourage doctors to invite patients' main concerns, avoid unnecessary interruptions, and acknowledge what matters most to the patient.

### B. Plain-Language Explanations

Use clear, understandable language and avoid unnecessary medical jargon.

### C. Opportunity for Questions

Create an explicit opportunity for patients to ask questions before the consultation ends.

### D. Teach-Back

Where appropriate, invite patients to explain key instructions in their own words so misunderstandings can be identified and corrected.

### E. Communication Support Materials

Provide concise, accessible written or visual information when it supports understanding.

Interventions should be selected based on local patient feedback, workflow analysis, and clinical feasibility.

## 11. PDSA Implementation Strategy

### PDSA Cycle 1 — Small-Scale Test

**Plan:** Select one communication practice and test it with a small number of consenting, eligible consultations, subject to local requirements.

**Do:** Implement the change and record practical observations.

**Study:** Review patient feedback, process adherence, consultation time, and staff observations.

**Act:** Adopt, adapt, or abandon the change based on findings.

### PDSA Cycle 2 — Refined Test

Modify the intervention based on Cycle 1 findings and test it in a broader but still manageable setting.

### PDSA Cycle 3 — Reliability and Sustainability

Test whether the refined practice can be consistently maintained within routine workflow.

The number and timing of cycles should depend on learning rather than a fixed requirement to complete exactly three cycles.

## 13. Risks and Mitigation

| Potential Risk | Mitigation Strategy |
|---|---|
| Low patient response rate | Use accessible, convenient, and voluntary feedback methods |
| Social desirability bias | Collect feedback independently where feasible and protect confidentiality |
| Staff resistance | Use supportive, non-punitive engagement and shared learning |
| Increased consultation time | Monitor balancing measures and adapt workflow |
| Inconsistent measurement | Standardise questions, definitions, and data collection |
| Privacy or confidentiality breaches | Use approved secure data handling and publish only de-identified aggregate findings |
| Limited generalisability | Clearly document the setting, sample, limitations, and context |

## 14. Ethical and Data Protection Considerations

- Obtain organisational permissions and any applicable ethical or institutional review determination before collecting data.
- Explain participation and consent requirements appropriately.
- Ensure that refusal to participate does not affect patient care.
- Avoid collecting unnecessary personal identifiers.
- Store sensitive data only in approved, access-controlled systems.
- Do not upload patient-level identifiable data to GitHub.
- Report findings in a way that minimises re-identification risks.
- Respect applicable laws, institutional policies, and professional standards.

## 16. Sustainability and Scale-Up

If local testing demonstrates useful and sustainable improvements, organisations may consider:

- Incorporating effective communication practices into routine clinical workflows.
- Including patient experience indicators in regular quality review meetings.
- Providing ongoing staff orientation and supportive feedback.
- Maintaining channels for patients to report communication concerns.
- Periodically reviewing measures for equity, reliability, and relevance.
- Testing adaptation in additional services before wider implementation.

## 17. Evaluation and Learning

Evaluation should consider:

- Whether the selected aim was achieved.
- The direction and stability of patient experience trends.
- Whether changes were implemented reliably.
- Potential unintended consequences.
- Feedback from patients and healthcare professionals.
- Contextual factors affecting implementation.
- Limitations of the measurement and evaluation approach.

A before-and-after change alone should not be treated as proof of causation.
