# Doctor–Patient Communication: Patient Experience Survey Questionnaire

## Patient Voice for Quality Care

**Document Type:** Patient-Reported Experience Survey Template  
**Project:** Improving Doctor–Patient Communication Through Patient Voice  
**Version:** 1.0 — Proposed Model  
**Status:** Draft for Local Adaptation and Pilot Testing  
**Suggested Setting:** Hospital Outpatient Department (OPD)  
**Estimated Completion Time:** 3–5 minutes, subject to local pilot testing  
**Instrument Status:** Not Yet Validated

---

## 1. Purpose of the Questionnaire

This questionnaire is designed to capture patients' experiences of communication with doctors during outpatient consultations.

It focuses on whether patients:

- Felt listened to and respected.
- Received understandable explanations.
- Had opportunities to ask questions.
- Understood their treatment instructions and next steps.
- Felt involved in discussions about their care.

The questionnaire supports the Patient Voice for Quality Care initiative by helping healthcare organisations identify communication gaps, monitor improvement, and use patient feedback to guide quality improvement interventions.

This tool measures patients' reported experiences. It does not directly assess diagnostic accuracy, clinical competence, or treatment effectiveness.

## 2. Intended Users

This questionnaire may be adapted by:

- Hospital quality improvement teams.
- Outpatient departments.
- Primary healthcare facilities.
- Patient experience teams.
- Healthcare quality researchers.
- Patient-centred care programmes.

The questionnaire should be reviewed for local language, cultural relevance, accessibility, and clinical context before implementation.

## 3. Instructions for Healthcare Organisations

Before administering the survey:

1. Obtain relevant organisational permissions and applicable ethics or institutional review determinations.
2. Define the target population and sampling method.
3. Translate and culturally adapt the questionnaire where necessary.
4. Conduct cognitive interviews or other appropriate pretesting with patients to assess comprehension.
5. Pilot the questionnaire and refine wording or administration procedures.
6. Train data collectors to use a consistent, neutral approach.
7. Establish a secure process for data storage and analysis.
8. Document any changes to questions, response options, or scoring.

Patient participation should be voluntary and should not influence access to care.

## 4. Patient Information and Introduction

### Suggested Patient Introduction

Dear Patient,

We would like to learn about your experience of communicating with the doctor during today's consultation.

Your feedback will help us understand what is working well and what could be improved.

Participation is voluntary. You may skip any question or choose not to participate. Your decision will not affect your healthcare.

Please do not write your name, telephone number, or other personal identifying information on this questionnaire.

Your responses will be handled confidentially in accordance with the healthcare organisation's approved procedures.

Thank you for helping us improve patient care.

## 7. Core Indicator Mapping

Three questionnaire items directly correspond to the primary patient-reported indicators in the project Measurement Plan.

| Measurement Plan Indicator | Survey Question | Positive Responses |
|---|---|---|
| M1 — Understandable Explanation Rate | Q4 | Very easy; Somewhat easy |
| M2 — Active Listening Experience Rate | Q1 | Always; Usually |
| M3 — Opportunity to Ask Questions Rate | Q7 | Yes |

The remaining questions provide supplementary diagnostic and improvement information.

M4 and M5 require separate process measurement methods, as specified in the Measurement Plan.

## 8. Scoring and Indicator Calculation

### M1 — Understandable Explanation Rate

**Numerator:** Number of respondents selecting "Very easy" or "Somewhat easy" for Q4.

**Denominator:** Number of respondents selecting one of the five substantive ease/difficulty responses for Q4.

**Formula:**

M1 (%) = (Q4 positive responses / Q4 valid substantive responses) × 100

Exclude missing responses and "Not applicable / No explanation was given" from the primary denominator, but report them separately.

### M2 — Active Listening Experience Rate

**Numerator:** Number of respondents selecting "Always" or "Usually" for Q1.

**Denominator:** Number of respondents selecting Always, Usually, Sometimes, or Never for Q1.

**Formula:**

M2 (%) = (Q1 positive responses / Q1 valid substantive responses) × 100

Exclude missing and Not applicable responses from the primary denominator and report them separately.

### M3 — Opportunity to Ask Questions Rate

**Numerator:** Number of respondents selecting "Yes" for Q7.

**Denominator:** Number of respondents selecting "Yes" or "No" for Q7.

**Formula:**

M3 (%) = (Q7 Yes responses / Q7 valid Yes-or-No responses) × 100

Report "Cannot remember" and missing responses separately.

### Additional Questions

Q2, Q3, Q5, Q6, Q8, Q9, Q10, Q11, and Q12 should initially be reported as distributions of response categories.

Do not combine all questionnaire items into a single numerical score without an explicit scoring rationale and appropriate psychometric evaluation.

## 9. Suggested Data Collection Procedure

1. Identify eligible patients according to the approved sampling plan.
2. Approach patients after their clinical consultation.
3. Explain the purpose and voluntary nature of the survey.
4. Follow the locally approved consent process.
5. Offer an accessible format or neutral assistance where needed.
6. Allow patients to respond privately whenever feasible.
7. Avoid having the treating doctor collect responses.
8. Check questionnaires for completeness without pressuring patients to answer.
9. Store responses securely.
10. Prepare de-identified summaries for quality improvement review.

## 10. Sampling and Response Rate

The implementing organisation should establish a sampling strategy before collecting data.

Possible approaches include:

- Consecutive sampling during selected clinic sessions.
- Systematic sampling using a predefined interval.
- Stratified sampling where meaningful differences between services or patient groups need to be assessed.

The sampling plan should define eligibility, exclusions, repeat visits, recruitment times, and response rate monitoring.

**Response Rate (%) = (Completed eligible surveys / Eligible patients invited) × 100**

A suggested operational target of approximately 20–30 completed surveys per week may be feasible in some small QI pilots, but this is not a universal statistical sample size recommendation.

The appropriate sample size depends on the intended precision, patient volume, sampling method, and analysis objectives.

## 13. Qualitative Feedback Analysis

Open-ended responses should be reviewed using a consistent thematic coding approach.

Illustrative categories include:

- Attentive listening.
- Respect and courtesy.
- Clarity of explanation.
- Medical terminology.
- Opportunity to ask questions.
- Treatment instructions.
- Shared decision-making.
- Consultation time and communication barriers.
- Other emerging themes.

The QI team should review both positive feedback and improvement suggestions.

Illustrative patient quotations should not be fabricated or presented as real patient testimony.

Before publishing comments, remove or appropriately redact identifying and potentially identifying information.

## 14. Equity, Language, and Accessibility

The questionnaire should be accessible to patients with different communication needs.

Consider:

- Plain-language translations.
- Appropriate local dialects.
- Large-print versions.
- Interviewer support for patients with limited literacy.
- Accessible electronic formats.
- Appropriate communication support for patients with disabilities.

Translations should be reviewed and pretested rather than assumed equivalent.

Where subgroup analysis is appropriate, protect privacy and avoid disclosing small identifiable groups.

## 15. Data Quality and Reliability

To strengthen data quality:

- Use consistent question wording and response options.
- Train data collectors.
- Pilot survey administration.
- Avoid leading or suggestive explanations.
- Document incomplete responses.
- Monitor response rates.
- Review potential nonresponse and social desirability bias.
- Use consistent indicator definitions.
- Document any questionnaire revisions.
- Assess the effect of revisions on comparability over time.

## 16. Instrument Development and Validation

This questionnaire is a proposed quality improvement tool, not a formally validated PREMs instrument.

Before presenting it as a validated measure, appropriate evaluation may include:

- Content validity review.
- Patient cognitive interviews.
- Cultural and linguistic adaptation.
- Pilot testing.
- Assessment of response distributions and missingness.
- Reliability testing where conceptually appropriate.
- Construct validity evaluation where appropriate.
- Assessment of sensitivity to change.

Formal psychometric evaluation should be guided by the intended use of the questionnaire and the nature of its items.

## 17. Ethical and Confidentiality Safeguards

Healthcare organisations should:

1. Obtain necessary organisational permissions and ethics or institutional review determinations.
2. Explain the purpose of feedback collection.
3. Ensure voluntary participation.
4. Protect patient confidentiality.
5. Use approved secure data storage.
6. Restrict access to patient-level responses.
7. Avoid using individual survey responses for punitive staff assessment.
8. Apply appropriate retention and deletion policies.
9. Publish only appropriately de-identified, disclosure-reviewed results.
10. Follow applicable laws and institutional policies.

**No identifiable patient information should be uploaded to a public GitHub repository.**

## 18. Limitations

The questionnaire may be affected by:

- Recall bias.
- Social desirability bias.
- Selection bias.
- Nonresponse bias.
- Differences in health literacy.
- Language and cultural differences.
- Variation in clinical complexity.
- Interviewer influence.
- Limited psychometric evidence.

Survey findings should be interpreted alongside process measures, qualitative feedback, and clinical context.

Patient-reported communication experience should not be interpreted as a direct measure of clinical effectiveness.
