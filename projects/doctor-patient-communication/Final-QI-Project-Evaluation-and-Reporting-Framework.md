## 2. Background and Rationale

Effective doctor–patient communication contributes to patient-centred care, shared understanding, patient engagement, and healthcare quality.

However, communication quality is not always systematically measured through patients' reported experiences.

This project introduces a structured approach to collecting patient feedback, measuring communication performance, and translating findings into continuous quality improvement.

## 3. Project Aim and Objectives

### Overall SMART Aim

Within 12 weeks, increase the proportion of OPD patients reporting that their doctor's explanation was easy to understand by at least 15 percentage points from the established baseline.

The target should be reviewed for feasibility after baseline measurement.

### Specific Objectives

1. Establish baseline communication performance.
2. Measure three patient-reported communication indicators.
3. Identify priority gaps and contributing factors.
4. Test communication improvement interventions using PDSA cycles.
5. Monitor outcome, process, and balancing measures.
6. Evaluate the feasibility, effectiveness signals, and sustainability of the improvement approach.

## 5. Evaluation Framework

| Evaluation Dimension | Key Question | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | Did the intervention address an identified communication gap? | Baseline findings and root cause analysis |
| Implementation | Were planned interventions delivered as intended? | PDSA documentation and process measures |
| Outcomes | Did patient-reported communication experience improve? | M1, M2, M3 and run charts |
| Balancing Effects | Were there unintended consequences? | Consultation duration, waiting time, staff feedback |
| Equity | Were communication needs addressed across patient groups? | Available, appropriately collected disaggregated findings |
| Sustainability | Can effective changes be maintained? | Staff ownership, workflow integration and monitoring plans |
| Transferability | Could the approach be adapted to other OPDs? | Feasibility findings and contextual requirements |
