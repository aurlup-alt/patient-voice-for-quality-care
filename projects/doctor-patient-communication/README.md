# Improving Doctor–Patient Communication Through Patient Voice

### A Practical, Patient-Centred Healthcare Quality Improvement Toolkit

**Part of:** [Patient Voice for Quality Care](../../README.md)  
**Project Type:** Adaptable Quality Improvement Model  
**Methodology:** Model for Improvement and Plan–Do–Study–Act (PDSA) Cycles  
**Suggested Setting:** Hospital Outpatient Department (OPD)  
**Suggested Duration:** 12 Weeks  
**Status:** Open-Source Toolkit — Proposed Model, Not Yet Implemented

---

## 1. About This Project

Effective communication between doctors and patients is a fundamental component of safe, respectful, equitable, and patient-centred healthcare.

Patients should have opportunities to express their concerns, receive clear explanations, ask questions, understand treatment instructions, and participate appropriately in decisions about their care.

The **Improving Doctor–Patient Communication Through Patient Voice** project provides an adaptable quality improvement framework that healthcare organisations can use to assess communication experiences, identify improvement opportunities, test practical interventions, and monitor change over time.

The central principle is:

**Listen to patients, understand their experiences, translate feedback into action, and measure whether care is improving.**

This project is designed as a reusable implementation model rather than a report of completed research or demonstrated clinical outcomes.

## 2. Project Objectives

The toolkit aims to help healthcare organisations:

1. Measure patients' experiences of doctor–patient communication.
2. Identify communication gaps through structured patient feedback.
3. Engage healthcare professionals and patients in improvement planning.
4. Test practical communication interventions through PDSA cycles.
5. Monitor patient experience, implementation processes, and unintended consequences.
6. Document learning and support sustainable improvements.

## 3. Who Can Use This Toolkit?

The toolkit may be adapted by:

- Hospital quality improvement teams.
- Outpatient departments and specialist clinics.
- Primary healthcare organisations.
- Patient experience and patient safety teams.
- Healthcare managers and clinical leaders.
- Quality improvement educators and researchers.
- Healthcare quality improvement networks.

Organisations should adapt the materials to their local clinical context, resources, languages, governance requirements, and patient populations.

## 5. How to Use the Toolkit

### Step 1 — Review the Project Charter

Start with the [Project Charter](Project-Charter.md).

Define the local clinical service, improvement problem, project sponsor, QI team, patient involvement, scope, and proposed timeline.

Do not assume that communication problems have a particular prevalence before collecting baseline information.

### Step 2 — Establish Governance and Permissions

Obtain appropriate organisational permissions and any required ethical or institutional review determinations.

Confirm arrangements for patient participation, data protection, secure storage, and reporting.

### Step 3 — Adapt the Survey Questionnaire

Review the [Patient Experience Survey Questionnaire](Doctor-Patient-Communication-Survey.md).

Adapt language and administration methods as needed. Conduct appropriate patient pretesting and a local pilot before routine implementation.

The survey is a proposed PREM-style tool and should not be described as a formally validated PREMs instrument without appropriate evaluation.

### Step 4 — Develop the Measurement Plan

Use the [Measurement Plan](Measurement-Plan.md) to define:

- Outcome indicators.
- Process indicators.
- Balancing measures.
- Eligibility criteria.
- Sampling and response-rate procedures.
- Numerator and denominator rules.
- Reporting frequency.
- Baseline and improvement targets.

### Step 5 — Collect Baseline Patient Feedback

Collect patient experience data before introducing the selected communication interventions.

Use a consistent sampling strategy and maintain patient confidentiality.

Review both quantitative responses and qualitative comments to identify priority improvement opportunities.

### Step 6 — Identify and Test Changes

Select practical changes based on baseline findings and frontline experience.

Potential interventions include:

- Active listening.
- Plain-language explanations.
- Encouraging patient questions.
- Checking understanding through teach-back when appropriate.
- Providing accessible communication support materials.

Test changes on a small scale through PDSA cycles before considering broader implementation.

### Step 7 — Monitor Indicators

Use the Excel dashboard or another locally approved system to record and review measurements over time.

Examine patient-reported outcomes together with process and balancing measures.

Avoid interpreting a single improved percentage as sufficient evidence of sustained improvement.

### Step 8 — Evaluate and Share Learning

Review trends, implementation fidelity, workflow effects, patient feedback, equity considerations, and project limitations.

Document what was tested, what changed, what was learned, and which interventions may be suitable for continued use.

Publish only appropriately de-identified and disclosure-reviewed findings.

## 6. Core Patient-Reported Outcome Indicators

The project includes three core patient-reported communication indicators.

| ID | Indicator | Survey Item | Positive Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Understandable Explanation Rate | Q4 | Very easy or Somewhat easy |
| M2 | Active Listening Experience Rate | Q1 | Always or Usually |
| M3 | Opportunity to Ask Questions Rate | Q7 | Yes |

Each percentage is calculated using the corresponding valid-response denominator defined in the Measurement Plan.

Missing, not-applicable, and uncertain responses should be handled according to the documented indicator specifications.

## 7. Additional Process and Balancing Measures

Patient experience scores alone do not demonstrate whether a communication intervention was implemented consistently.

The project therefore recommends:

**Process Measures**

- M4 — Teach-Back Implementation Rate.
- M5 — Communication Practice Adherence Rate.

**Balancing Measures**

- M6 — Average Consultation Duration.
- M7 — Staff-Reported Workflow Burden.

M4–M7 require locally defined data collection processes beyond the core patient survey dashboard unless additional functionality has been developed and verified.

## 8. Suggested 12-Week Implementation Roadmap

| Period | Activities | Expected Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Team formation, project scope, governance, survey adaptation, measurement planning | Local Project Charter and Measurement Plan |
| Weeks 3–4 | Baseline patient experience assessment | Baseline findings and priority gaps |
| Weeks 5–6 | First PDSA test | Initial intervention learning |
| Weeks 7–8 | Refined PDSA testing | Adapted communication practices |
| Weeks 9–10 | Continued measurement and evaluation | Trend analysis and balancing review |
| Weeks 11–12 | Sustainability planning and documentation | QI report and recommendations |

This timeline is illustrative. Organisations may need to adjust it according to local approvals, staffing, patient volume, and implementation readiness.

## 9. Using the Excel QI Dashboard

The [Excel Data Collection and QI Dashboard](Patient_Voice_Doctor_Patient_Communication_QI_Dashboard.xlsx) is designed to support routine measurement.

Suggested workflow:

1. Download the workbook.
2. Read the Instructions and Indicator Dictionary worksheets.
3. Verify the workbook structure, formulas, and response categories.
4. Test the workbook using clearly labelled synthetic example records in a separate local copy.
5. Remove test records before actual use.
6. Enter approved survey responses into the Data Entry worksheet.
7. Review calculated indicators and weekly summaries.
8. Examine the Dashboard for trends.
9. Check missing responses, exclusions, and denominators.
10. Save operational data only in an approved secure environment.

**Do not upload a completed patient-level workbook to this public repository.**

The workbook should be independently checked before clinical or operational use. Its inclusion in the toolkit does not constitute certification of calculation accuracy.

## 10. Patient Participation and Ethical Safeguards

All implementation activities should follow applicable legal, institutional, and ethical requirements.

Key safeguards include:

- Voluntary patient participation.
- Appropriate consent or participation procedures.
- Confidential handling of survey responses.
- Minimum necessary data collection.
- Secure storage and restricted access.
- Protection against re-identification.
- Non-punitive use of patient feedback.
- Accessible participation for patients with different communication needs.
- Appropriate institutional permissions and ethics review determinations.

Public GitHub repositories must not contain identifiable patient-level information.

## 11. Interpretation and Limitations

This toolkit is intended to support quality improvement, not to establish causal effectiveness through its design alone.

Users should consider:

- Sampling and nonresponse bias.
- Survey comprehension and validity.
- Differences in clinical context.
- Small sample sizes.
- Changes in survey administration.
- Variation in implementation fidelity.
- Unintended consequences.
- Limitations of before-and-after comparisons.

Any claims of effectiveness should be supported by appropriate data, analysis, and transparent reporting.
