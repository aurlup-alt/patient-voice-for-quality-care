# Contributing to Patient Voice for Quality Care

## 1. Welcome

Thank you for your interest in contributing to Patient Voice for Quality Care.

This project aims to help healthcare organisations listen to patients, understand their experiences, and use patient feedback to improve healthcare quality, safety, equity, and patient-centred care.

We welcome contributions from healthcare professionals, quality improvement practitioners, researchers, patient safety specialists, patients, caregivers, and patient representatives from different countries and healthcare settings.

You do not need to be an expert in every aspect of patient experience measurement to contribute. Practical experience, constructive feedback, and thoughtful suggestions are valuable.

## 2. What Can You Contribute?

Contributions may include:

- **Practical tools:** Patient experience surveys, interview guides, feedback forms, and improvement worksheets.
- **Measurement resources:** PREMs indicators, measurement plans, data collection guidance, and analysis methods.
- **Quality improvement resources:** PDSA worksheets, action plans, monitoring tools, and implementation guides.
- **Case studies:** Examples of how patient feedback has informed service improvement.
- **Evidence and research:** Relevant publications, guidelines, systematic reviews, and evidence-informed recommendations.
- **Patient and caregiver perspectives:** Suggestions that help ensure resources reflect the needs and experiences of people receiving care.
- **Accessibility and equity:** Recommendations for engaging people with disabilities, language barriers, or other barriers to participation.
- **Translations:** Adaptations that make resources more accessible across languages and healthcare settings.
- **Corrections and improvements:** Identification of unclear wording, technical errors, limitations, or opportunities to improve existing resources.

All contributions should support the project's focus on using patient voice to improve healthcare quality.

## 3. Principles for Contributions

Please ensure that contributions:

1. Respect patients' dignity, autonomy, privacy, and rights.
2. Recognise patients as partners in healthcare improvement.
3. Use clear, inclusive, and accessible language.
4. Distinguish evidence-based recommendations from local experience or opinion.
5. Avoid unsupported claims about effectiveness.
6. Consider differences across healthcare settings, cultures, and patient groups.
7. Protect confidential and personally identifiable information.
8. Support learning and improvement rather than blame or punishment.

Where appropriate, explain the context in which a tool or recommendation has been used.

## 4. Before Submitting a Contribution

Before proposing a new resource:

- Review the existing repository to check whether a similar resource already exists.
- Identify the problem the proposed resource is intended to address.
- Explain who may benefit from it.
- Provide references for evidence-based claims where available.
- Identify any limitations, assumptions, or local adaptations.
- Ensure that you have the right to share any submitted material.

Please do not upload patient records, identifiable patient stories, confidential organisational documents, or copyrighted material without appropriate permission.

## 5. How to Contribute Through GitHub

### Option A: Report an Issue or Suggest an Improvement

1. Open the repository's Issues section.
2. Describe the proposed change or problem clearly.
3. Explain why the change would improve the resource.
4. Include relevant evidence or examples where appropriate.
5. Allow the maintainers to review and discuss the suggestion.

Repository: https://github.com/aurlup-alt/patient-voice-for-quality-care

If GitHub Issues are not enabled, contact the repository maintainer through an available public contact channel or submit a pull request.

### Option B: Submit a Pull Request

If you are familiar with GitHub:

1. Fork the repository.
2. Create a branch for your proposed change.
3. Add or revise the relevant Markdown file.
4. Review the content for accuracy, clarity, accessibility, and privacy.
5. Commit your changes with a clear message.
6. Submit a pull request describing the change and its rationale.
7. Respond to questions or suggestions during review.

Please keep each pull request focused on a clearly defined improvement.

### Option C: Contribute Without Technical Experience

You can still contribute by:

- Suggesting a new resource.
- Reviewing a template and identifying unclear questions.
- Sharing general lessons from a quality improvement project.
- Recommending relevant evidence or guidance.
- Providing feedback on accessibility and usability.
- Suggesting translations or local adaptations.

Describe your suggestion clearly. The repository maintainer can help determine the most appropriate way to incorporate it.

## 6. Suggested Format for New Resources

Where appropriate, new resources should include:

- **Title:** A clear and descriptive name.
- **Purpose:** What the resource is intended to achieve.
- **Target users:** Who should use it.
- **How to use it:** Practical instructions.
- **Template or tool:** The main content.
- **Measurement approach:** Relevant indicators or evaluation methods.
- **Ethical considerations:** Privacy, consent, confidentiality, and accessibility.
- **Limitations:** Important caveats or contexts where the resource may not apply.
- **References:** Supporting evidence or guidance, where available.
- **Version or review information:** Where useful for maintaining the resource over time.

Not every contribution requires every section. The format should remain proportionate to the purpose of the resource.

## 7. Evidence and References

We encourage evidence-informed contributions.

When making research-based recommendations:

- Cite relevant and accessible sources.
- Prefer high-quality research, systematic reviews, recognised guidance, and validated measurement instruments where appropriate.
- Identify the source and version of a tool when known.
- Avoid describing a locally developed questionnaire as validated unless appropriate validation evidence exists.
- Explain when evidence is limited or findings may not generalise to other settings.
- Distinguish between published evidence, local experience, and proposed ideas.

A reference supports transparency; it does not automatically establish that a particular intervention will work in every setting.

## 8. Patient Privacy and Ethical Responsibilities

Patient voice resources must be developed and shared responsibly.

Contributors must not submit identifiable patient information, medical records, or confidential material.

When sharing case studies or patient quotations:

- Remove information that could identify a patient.
- Obtain appropriate consent and permissions where required.
- Follow applicable laws and organisational policies.
- Address ethics review or approval requirements where applicable.
- Avoid examples that could expose patients or staff to harm, stigma, or discrimination.

If you are uncertain whether material can be shared, seek appropriate guidance before submitting it.

## 9. Language and Cultural Adaptation

Resources may be used in different languages, countries, and healthcare systems.

When translating or adapting a resource:

- Preserve the intended meaning of the original.
- Use language patients can understand.
- Consider local terminology and cultural context.
- Avoid assumptions that one healthcare model applies everywhere.
- Identify substantial changes from the original version.
- Do not claim that a translated instrument is validated without supporting evidence.

Translations of validated questionnaires may require permission and formal linguistic or cultural validation. Check the instrument's terms of use before adapting or redistributing it.

## 10. Review and Acceptance

Contributions may be reviewed for:

- Relevance to the project's purpose.
- Clarity and practical usefulness.
- Accuracy and appropriate use of evidence.
- Ethical and privacy considerations.
- Accessibility and inclusiveness.
- Duplication of existing resources.
- Suitability for use across different healthcare settings.

Maintainers may request revisions, suggest combining similar resources, or decline contributions that do not meet these principles.

Submitting a contribution does not guarantee acceptance or publication.

## 11. Copyright and Licensing

Before contributing, ensure that you have the right to share the material.

The repository's licence applies to material covered by that licence, subject to its terms and any applicable third-party rights.

Do not assume that third-party questionnaires, publications, images, or instruments can be copied or redistributed freely. Check the relevant licence and permissions.

Where a contribution includes material with separate licensing conditions, clearly identify those conditions.

## 12. Respectful Collaboration

We aim to maintain a welcoming and constructive environment.

All contributors are expected to:

- Communicate respectfully.
- Welcome different professional and patient perspectives.
- Critique ideas and methods rather than attack individuals.
- Explain disagreements constructively.
- Respect confidentiality.
- Avoid discriminatory, harassing, or abusive behaviour.

Patient experience is shaped by complex interactions among individuals, teams, organisations, and health systems. Contributions should support constructive learning.

## 15. Our Shared Goal

Patient voice should be more than a survey, a complaint form, or a reporting requirement.

It should help healthcare organisations understand what matters to patients, act on their experiences, and learn whether care has improved.

Every thoughtful contribution can help advance that goal.

**Listen to patients. Learn together. Improve care.**
