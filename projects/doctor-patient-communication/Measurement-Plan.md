# Quality Improvement Measurement Plan

## Improving Doctor–Patient Communication Through Patient Voice

**Project:** Patient Voice for Quality Care  
**Document Type:** Adaptable QI Measurement Plan  
**Version:** 1.0 — Proposed Model  
**Status:** Template / Not Yet Implemented  
**Suggested Setting:** Hospital Outpatient Department  
**QI Methodology:** Model for Improvement and PDSA Cycles  
**Suggested Project Duration:** 12 Weeks

---

## 1. Purpose

This Measurement Plan provides a structured approach to assessing, monitoring, and improving doctor–patient communication through patient-reported experience data and complementary process and balancing measures.

It is designed for healthcare organisations that wish to implement a patient-centred quality improvement project and evaluate whether changes in clinical communication practices lead to measurable improvement.

The framework is adaptable to different clinical settings, patient populations, and organisational capacities.

No actual patient data, baseline findings, or improvement outcomes are presented in this document.

## 2. Measurement Objectives

1. Establish a reliable baseline of patients' experiences of doctor–patient communication.
2. Monitor patient-reported communication outcomes over time.
3. Assess whether selected communication interventions are being implemented consistently.
4. Identify potential unintended consequences affecting clinical workflow.
5. Detect differences in communication experiences across patient groups where appropriate.
6. Use data to guide PDSA cycles and sustainability decisions.

## 3. Measurement Principles

The measurement approach should follow these principles:

- **Patient-centredness:** Give priority to what patients experience and understand.
- **Operational clarity:** Define every indicator consistently.
- **Feasibility:** Use methods that are practical in routine care.
- **Comparability:** Maintain stable measurement definitions over time.
- **Confidentiality:** Protect patient and clinician information.
- **Equity:** Consider whether communication improvements benefit different patient groups.
- **Actionability:** Use findings to inform specific improvement decisions.
- **Transparency:** Document limitations, missing data, and changes in measurement methods.

## 4. Measurement Framework

The framework includes three principal categories.

### A. Outcome Measures

Outcome measures assess the communication experience reported by patients.

- M1: Understandable Explanation Rate.
- M2: Active Listening Experience Rate.
- M3: Opportunity to Ask Questions Rate.

### B. Process Measures

Process measures assess whether selected communication practices are being implemented.

- M4: Teach-Back Implementation Rate.
- M5: Communication Practice Adherence Rate.

### C. Balancing Measures

Balancing measures assess whether the intervention creates unintended operational consequences.

- M6: Average Consultation Duration.
- M7: Staff-Reported Workflow Burden.

An additional equity analysis is recommended where feasible.

## 5. Indicator Specifications

### M1 — Understandable Explanation Rate

**Category:** Outcome measure  
**Priority:** Primary outcome indicator

**Operational Definition:** Percentage of eligible survey respondents reporting that the doctor's explanation of their condition, treatment, or next steps was easy to understand.

**Suggested Survey Question:**

"How easy was it to understand the doctor's explanation about your health condition or treatment?"

**Suggested Response Options:**

1. Very easy
2. Somewhat easy
3. Neither easy nor difficult
4. Somewhat difficult
5. Very difficult
6. Not applicable / No explanation was given

**Numerator:** Number of respondents selecting "Very easy" or "Somewhat easy."

**Denominator:** Number of respondents selecting one of the five substantive ease/difficulty responses.

**Calculation:**

Understandable Explanation Rate (%) = (Number of positive responses / Number of valid substantive responses) × 100

**Exclusions:** Missing responses and "Not applicable / No explanation was given" are excluded from the primary denominator and reported separately.

**Data Source:** Patient experience survey.

**Measurement Frequency:** Weekly.

**Interpretation:** A higher percentage indicates more favourable patient-reported understanding of explanations.

**Important Note:** Patients reporting that no explanation was given should be reviewed as a separate communication concern rather than silently ignored.

### M2 — Active Listening Experience Rate

**Category:** Outcome measure

**Operational Definition:** Percentage of eligible survey respondents reporting that their doctor listened carefully to their concerns.

**Suggested Survey Question:**

"During your consultation, how often did the doctor listen carefully to what you had to say?"

**Response Options:**

1. Always
2. Usually
3. Sometimes
4. Never
5. Not applicable

**Numerator:** Number of respondents selecting "Always" or "Usually."

**Denominator:** Number of respondents selecting Always, Usually, Sometimes, or Never.

**Calculation:**

Active Listening Experience Rate (%) = (Positive responses / Valid substantive responses) × 100

**Data Source:** Patient experience survey.

**Frequency:** Weekly.

**Interpretation:** Higher values indicate more favourable patient-reported listening experiences.

### M3 — Opportunity to Ask Questions Rate

**Category:** Outcome measure

**Operational Definition:** Percentage of eligible respondents reporting that they were given an opportunity to ask questions during their consultation.

**Suggested Survey Question:**

"Did the doctor give you an opportunity to ask questions about your health or treatment?"

**Response Options:**

1. Yes
2. No
3. Cannot remember

**Numerator:** Number of respondents selecting "Yes."

**Denominator:** Number of respondents selecting "Yes" or "No."

**Calculation:**

Opportunity to Ask Questions Rate (%) = (Yes responses / Valid Yes-or-No responses) × 100

**Data Source:** Patient experience survey.

**Frequency:** Weekly.

**Reporting Note:** "Cannot remember" and missing responses should be reported separately.

### M4 — Teach-Back Implementation Rate

**Category:** Process measure

**Operational Definition:** Percentage of eligible consultations in which a clinician appropriately checks the patient's understanding by inviting the patient to explain key instructions in their own words.

**Numerator:** Number of assessed eligible consultations in which teach-back was appropriately performed.

**Denominator:** Total number of assessed consultations in which teach-back was indicated according to locally defined criteria.

**Calculation:**

Teach-Back Implementation Rate (%) = (Consultations with appropriate teach-back / Eligible consultations assessed) × 100

**Data Source:** Approved structured observation or reliable clinical documentation.

**Frequency:** Weekly or per PDSA test.

**Measurement Consideration:** Define eligibility criteria, observer training, and documentation standards before data collection.

**Important:** Routine use of teach-back may not be appropriate in every consultation. Eligibility must be clinically and contextually defined.

### M5 — Communication Practice Adherence Rate

**Category:** Process measure

**Operational Definition:** Percentage of assessed eligible consultations in which all locally selected essential communication practices were completed.

**Illustrative Communication Bundle:**

- Invite the patient's main concern.
- Demonstrate attentive listening.
- Explain key information in understandable language.
- Offer an opportunity for questions.

**Numerator:** Number of assessed eligible consultations meeting every applicable element of the defined bundle.

**Denominator:** Number of eligible consultations assessed using the same bundle definition.

**Calculation:**

Communication Practice Adherence Rate (%) = (Fully adherent consultations / Eligible consultations assessed) × 100

**Data Source:** Approved structured observation or standardised documentation.

**Frequency:** Weekly or during PDSA cycles.

**Important:** Bundle elements, applicability rules, and data collection methods must be agreed upon before measurement. Do not infer adherence from patient satisfaction scores alone.

### M6 — Average Consultation Duration

**Category:** Balancing measure

**Operational Definition:** Mean elapsed time in minutes from the start to the end of a clinical consultation, using a consistent locally defined method.

**Numerator:** Total consultation minutes across observed eligible consultations.

**Denominator:** Number of eligible consultations with valid duration records.

**Calculation:**

Average Consultation Duration = Total Consultation Minutes / Number of Valid Consultations

**Data Source:** Time observation or an appropriately configured clinical information system.

**Frequency:** Weekly or during selected PDSA tests.

**Interpretation:** Assess changes alongside patient experience and workflow context. Longer or shorter consultations are not inherently better.

### M7 — Staff-Reported Workflow Burden

**Category:** Balancing measure

**Operational Definition:** Percentage of participating staff reporting that the tested communication intervention creates an unacceptable additional workload or workflow burden.

**Suggested Question:**

"During the past week, did the communication improvement intervention create an unacceptable additional workload or workflow burden for you?"

**Response Options:**

- Yes
- No
- Unsure

**Numerator:** Number of participating respondents selecting "Yes."

**Denominator:** Number selecting "Yes" or "No."

**Calculation:**

Workflow Burden Rate (%) = (Yes responses / Valid Yes-or-No responses) × 100

**Data Source:** Brief anonymous staff feedback questionnaire.

**Frequency:** Every two weeks or after major PDSA tests.

**Reporting Note:** Report the number of staff responding, missing responses, and "Unsure" responses separately.

## 7. Sampling Strategy

### Target Population

Patients receiving consultations in the selected clinical service during the measurement period.

### Suggested Approach

Use a feasible, predefined sampling strategy, such as systematic sampling or consecutive recruitment during selected clinic sessions.

The sampling plan should specify:

- Eligibility criteria.
- Recruitment location and timing.
- Sampling days and clinic sessions.
- Target number of completed surveys.
- Handling of repeat visits.
- Response rate monitoring.
- Procedures to minimise selection bias.

### Illustrative Planning Target

An initial operational target of approximately 20–30 completed patient surveys per week may be considered for a small pilot, subject to patient volume, feasibility, and the intended precision of estimates.

This is not a statistically justified universal sample size. Formal precision requirements or subgroup comparisons may require a larger sample.

### Response Rate

Response Rate (%) = (Completed eligible surveys / Eligible patients invited) × 100

Report the number approached, eligible, declining, and completing the survey where feasible.

## 8. Data Collection Procedures

1. Confirm applicable approvals and patient information requirements.
2. Orient staff responsible for data collection.
3. Pilot the questionnaire for clarity and feasibility.
4. Invite eligible patients using a consistent approach.
5. Obtain appropriate consent.
6. Collect feedback preferably after the consultation.
7. Avoid collection by the treating doctor when feasible to reduce response bias.
8. Record responses using a secure approved system.
9. Check data completeness and consistency.
10. Prepare de-identified weekly summaries.

Patient participation must be voluntary and must not affect care.

## 9. Baseline Measurement

The baseline phase should occur before the proposed communication intervention is introduced.

**Suggested Baseline Period:** Weeks 3–4 of the project, subject to feasibility.

For each indicator, record:

- Measurement dates.
- Number of eligible observations or respondents.
- Numerator.
- Denominator.
- Baseline percentage or mean.
- Missing or excluded responses.
- Relevant contextual observations.

## 10. Improvement Targets

Improvement targets should be:

- Based on measured baseline performance.
- Relevant to patient priorities.
- Feasible within the project duration.
- Agreed upon by the QI team.
- Reviewed for unintended consequences.

**Illustrative Target Statement:**

"Increase the Understandable Explanation Rate from [baseline X%] to [target Y%] by [date], without creating an unacceptable increase in consultation time or staff workload."

Do not use an arbitrary universal percentage as a mandatory performance standard.

## 11. Data Analysis and Visualisation

### Weekly Measurement

For each reporting period:

1. Calculate the indicator numerator and denominator.
2. Calculate the percentage or mean.
3. Plot results chronologically.
4. Annotate the timing of tested interventions.
5. Review changes alongside process and balancing measures.
6. Record important contextual factors.

### Recommended Charts

- Run charts for weekly patient experience indicators.
- Run charts for teach-back and communication bundle adherence.
- Run charts for consultation duration.
- Simple tables for response rates, missing data, and staff feedback.

Where sufficient data are available, teams may use appropriate statistical process control charts and established rules for distinguishing common-cause from special-cause variation.

A small number of weekly observations may be insufficient for reliable interpretation of changes over time.

## 12. Equity and Subgroup Analysis

Where appropriate, ethically permissible, and adequately supported by sample size, consider examining patient experience across:

- Age groups.
- Language preferences.
- Disability-related communication needs.
- First-time versus follow-up visits.
- Other locally relevant characteristics.

Avoid publishing small cells or combinations of characteristics that could identify individuals.

Subgroup differences should be interpreted cautiously, particularly when samples are small or recruitment is nonrepresentative.

## 13. Data Quality Assurance

The QI team should establish procedures for:

- Standardised indicator definitions.
- Consistent questionnaire administration.
- Training of data collectors.
- Prevention of duplicate entries.
- Verification of numerator and denominator calculations.
- Review of missing data and exclusions.
- Documentation of changes to survey wording.
- Secure storage and restricted access.
- Periodic checks of observation reliability.

If definitions or response categories change, document the change and assess whether earlier and later results remain comparable.

## 14. Measurement Review and Decision-Making

### Weekly QI Review

Review:

- Primary outcome indicator trends.
- Other patient experience measures.
- Process adherence.
- Balancing measures.
- Data completeness.
- Patient comments and staff observations.
- Findings from current PDSA tests.

### Decision Questions

- Is the tested change being implemented as intended?
- Are patients reporting a better communication experience?
- Are improvements consistent over time?
- Are there signs of adverse workflow effects?
- Should the intervention be adopted, adapted, or abandoned?
- Is additional testing needed?

## 16. Data Protection and Ethical Safeguards

- Obtain organisational and any required ethical approvals or determinations.
- Use the minimum necessary information.
- Explain the voluntary nature of patient participation.
- Avoid identifiable patient-level information in public repositories.
- Store raw responses in approved secure systems.
- Share only appropriately de-identified and disclosure-reviewed summaries.
- Apply appropriate retention and deletion policies.
- Follow relevant laws, institutional requirements, and professional guidance.

## 17. Reporting Outputs

The measurement process should produce:

1. Baseline Measurement Summary.
2. Weekly Indicator Dashboard.
3. Run Charts.
4. PDSA Measurement Notes.
5. Data Quality and Missingness Summary.
6. Final Measurement and Learning Report.

These outputs should support local decision-making and transparent quality improvement reporting.

## 18. Limitations

This framework is an illustrative quality improvement measurement model.

Potential limitations include:

- Selection and nonresponse bias.
- Social desirability bias.
- Limited survey validity in untested populations.
- Small sample sizes.
- Observer effects.
- Differences in clinical case mix.
- Variation in data collection practices.
- Inability to establish causation from simple before-and-after comparisons.

Measurement tools should be locally piloted and, where appropriate, formally validated before being presented as validated PREMs instruments.
