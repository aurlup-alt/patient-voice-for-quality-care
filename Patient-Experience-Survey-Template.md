# Patient Experience Survey Template

## 1. Purpose

This template provides a practical framework for collecting patient feedback about their experiences of healthcare services. It is designed to help healthcare organisations identify service gaps, understand patients' perspectives, and translate feedback into meaningful quality improvement actions.

The survey may be adapted for hospitals, outpatient departments, primary healthcare facilities, diagnostic centres, and other healthcare settings.

**Important:** This is a general-purpose survey template, not a validated PREMs instrument. Pilot-test it in the intended setting before routine use.

## 7. Suggested Analysis Plan

### A. Quantitative Analysis

For each question, calculate:

- Number of valid responses.
- Number of responses marked N/A.
- Distribution of responses across rating categories.
- Percentage of respondents selecting each category.
- Mean or median score, where appropriate.

For questions using a 1–5 scale, a positive-experience percentage may be calculated as:

**Positive-experience percentage = (Number of valid responses rated 4 or 5 ÷ Total valid responses) × 100**

Exclude N/A and missing responses from the denominator. Report the denominator alongside each percentage.

Do not assume that the response scale is validated or that a high score proves that care is safe or clinically effective.

### B. Qualitative Analysis

Review open-ended responses and group them into recurring themes, such as:

- Waiting time and access.
- Communication and information.
- Respect and dignity.
- Privacy.
- Involvement in decisions.
- Staff responsiveness.
- Continuity and coordination of care.
- Environment and facilities.

Document both positive experiences and reported problems. Where possible, use a consistent coding approach and have a second reviewer check a sample of responses.

### C. Interpretation

When interpreting results:

- Consider the number of responses and response rate.
- Recognise potential non-response and selection bias.
- Compare results over time only when the survey method and population are sufficiently comparable.
- Avoid overinterpreting small differences or small samples.
- Review patterns across relevant service areas without exposing individuals.
- Combine patient feedback with other quality and safety information.

## 9. From Patient Feedback to Quality Improvement

Survey findings should lead to action rather than simply being collected and reported.

Use the following sequence:

1. Identify the most important patient-reported problem.
2. Validate the finding with relevant staff and available service data.
3. Engage patients or patient representatives in understanding the problem, where feasible.
4. Define a specific, measurable improvement aim.
5. Test a small change using the Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA) approach.
6. Monitor whether patient experience improves and check for unintended consequences.
7. Share the findings and explain what actions were taken.

### Example

**Finding:** Patients report that staff do not clearly explain expected waiting times.

**Possible action:** Pilot a simple waiting-time information process in one outpatient department.

**Process measure:** Percentage of sampled patients who report receiving an explanation of expected waiting time.

**Outcome measure:** Percentage of surveyed patients rating waiting-time communication as good or very good.

**Balancing measure:** Staff workload or delays associated with the new process.

This is an illustrative example. The improvement team should establish a baseline and set a locally appropriate target before testing the change.

## 10. Ethical and Practical Considerations

- Participation should be voluntary and should not affect care.
- Explain the purpose of the survey and how responses will be used.
- Use accessible language and provide assistance when needed without influencing responses.
- Avoid pressuring patients to give positive feedback.
- Offer appropriate privacy during interviews or survey completion.
- Follow applicable consent, ethics review, and data protection requirements.
- Establish a process for responding to urgent safety concerns disclosed through feedback.
- Do not publish identifiable patient comments or sensitive information.
- Be transparent about limitations and about changes that have or have not been made.

## 11. Limitations

This template is a starting point for local adaptation. It has not been psychometrically validated and should not automatically be treated as a standardised or validated PREMs instrument.

Before wider implementation, assess question clarity, relevance, accessibility, cultural appropriateness, feasibility, and reliability as appropriate. If the purpose is formal research, benchmarking, or comparison across facilities, consider a validated instrument and obtain the necessary approvals.
