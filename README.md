# Patient Voice for Quality Care

**Practical tools to listen to patients, understand their experiences, and improve healthcare quality.**

## About this project

Patient Voice for Quality Care is an open resource initiative to help healthcare professionals, quality improvement teams, researchers, and healthcare organisations systematically collect, analyse, and use patient experience feedback to improve patient-centred care.

Patient feedback should not end with data collection. It should inform meaningful changes in communication, access, dignity, responsiveness, safety, and the overall experience of care.

## Objectives

- Support systematic collection of patient experience feedback.
- Help teams analyse quantitative and qualitative feedback.
- Translate patient-identified concerns into measurable quality improvement actions.
- Promote respectful, inclusive, and patient-centred healthcare.
- Encourage transparent feedback loops so patients can see how their input informs improvement.

## Available resources

Use the following practical tools as starting points. Adapt them to your local context and applicable ethical, legal, and organisational requirements.

1. **[Patient Experience Survey Template](Patient-Experience-Survey-Template.md)** — a starting point for gathering feedback about patients' experiences of care.
2. **[PREMs Measurement Guide](PREMs-Measurement-Guide.md)** — guidance on patient-reported experience measures, sampling, measurement, and interpretation.
3. **[Patient Feedback Analysis Template](Patient-Feedback-Analysis-Template.md)** — a structured approach to reviewing feedback, identifying themes, and prioritising improvement opportunities.
4. **[Patient Voice Indicator Template](Patient-Voice-Indicator-Template.md)** — a framework for defining indicators, numerators, denominators, baselines, targets, and data sources.
5. **[Patient Voice Improvement Action Plan](Patient-Voice-Improvement-Action-Plan.md)** — a practical way to connect patient feedback with improvement actions and follow-up measurement.
6. **[Patient Voice Case Study Template](Patient-Voice-Case-Study-Template.md)** — a structure for documenting improvement work, results, limitations, and lessons learned.

## How to use the resources

1. Define the care setting and the question you want to understand.
2. Collect patient feedback using an appropriate method.
3. Review the feedback for recurring themes, inequalities, and potential safety concerns.
4. Involve patients and staff in understanding the underlying causes.
5. Select a specific improvement opportunity and define measurable aims.
6. Test changes on a small scale using Plan–Do–Study–Act (PDSA) cycles where appropriate.
7. Monitor results, review unintended effects, and share learning with patients and staff.

## First quality improvement focus: Doctor–patient communication

An initial improvement project may focus on whether patients feel listened to, receive understandable explanations, have opportunities to ask questions, and understand the next steps in their care.

The project should begin with local baseline measurement and patient input. Improvement targets should be set after the baseline is understood rather than assumed in advance.

## Important considerations

- **Locally developed tools:** The survey template is a starting point, not a validated patient-reported experience measure. Review, adapt, and pilot it before wider use.
- **Ethics and consent:** Follow applicable organisational requirements for consent, ethical review, and data collection.
- **Privacy:** Do not publish identifiable patient information, medical records, or confidential organisational data in this repository.
- **Equity and accessibility:** Consider language, disability, literacy, digital access, and the experiences of underserved groups.
- **Measurement:** Distinguish patient experience measures (PREMs) from patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs). They answer different questions.
- **Interpretation:** Feedback is valuable, but results should be interpreted in context and should not be treated as representative of all patients unless the sampling approach supports that conclusion.

## Who can use this project?

Healthcare professionals, quality improvement practitioners, patient safety teams, researchers, students, patient representatives, and healthcare organisations interested in improving patient-centred care.
